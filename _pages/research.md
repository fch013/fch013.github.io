My work brings optical hardware, forward models, calibration, and computational inference into one end-to-end imaging design process. The goal is to recover useful biological information beyond conventional trade-offs in speed, depth, and resolution.

## Current projects

### Physics-Informed Deep Learning for Light-Field Microscopy

I am developing reconstruction methods that embed the physical light-field forward model—including microlens sampling, aberrations, and measurement multiplexing—into learning for robust, high-speed volumetric bioimaging.

Related manuscript: **Richardson-Lucy-Based Deconvolution Network for Light Field Microscopy** (in preparation).

### 3D Computer-Generated Holographic Stimulation With Temporal Focusing

This work combines temporal-focusing propagation and a digital propagation matrix with learning-based hologram synthesis for rapid 3D multiphoton stimulation and improved axial confinement.

Related publication: [Deep-Computer-Generated Holography With Temporal Focusing and a Digital Propagation Matrix for Rapid 3D Multiphoton Stimulation](https://doi.org/10.1364/OE.505956), *Optics Express* (2024).

### Vectorial Temporal-Focusing Simulation

I am developing vectorial spatiotemporal models of DMD-based line-scanning temporal focusing to connect illumination geometry, pulse propagation, and system parameters with excitation performance, calibration, and imaging through scattering media.

Related manuscript: **Vectorial Spatiotemporal Modeling of Line-Scanning Temporal Focusing** (in preparation).

### Adaptive Optics and Wavefront Sensing

I develop compact sensing and reconstruction methods that embed optical constraints directly into learning, including joint recovery of wavefront and beam intensity from microlens-array measurements.

Related publication: [Microlens Array-Based Beam Profile and Wavefront Sensor With Physical Constraint Learning](https://doi.org/10.1109/JPHOT.2025.3561931), *IEEE Photonics Journal* (2025).

## Research experience

As a visiting scholar in Mechanical Engineering with Prof. Peter T. C. So, I worked on high-speed computational multiphoton imaging through scattering media, including DMD line-scanning temporal focusing and silicon-photomultiplier detection.
